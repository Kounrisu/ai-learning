# AI Learning

A public notebook for understanding AI, using current AI tools well, and keeping up with meaningful changes.

## Learning goals

- Explain AI, machine learning, deep learning, and generative AI.
- Understand frontier AI, how it is evaluated, and where evidence is uncertain.
- Build practical skill with Claude, ChatGPT, Grok and Grok's bot features, and Gemini.
- Follow important model, product, and capability releases.
- Keep reusable prompts and workflows, and learn from researchers and practitioners.

## Study paths

1. **Foundations** — training and inference, tokens, context windows, tools, multimodality, and common limitations.
2. **Frontier models** — capabilities, evaluations, reasoning, agents, safety, costs, and access; separate measured results from claims.
3. **Tool guides** — setup, strengths, limitations, privacy controls, and repeatable workflows for each assistant.
4. **Practice** — small exercises for research, writing, coding, learning, and everyday tasks.
5. **What's next** — releases and research that could change how people use AI.

## Keeping this current

For each release, save the date, official source, what changed, who can use it, and a short hands-on check. Mark analysis as analysis; link to primary sources. Do not treat an announcement or social post as independent proof.

## Prompts and workflows

Store prompts with their purpose, inputs, expected output, tool/model tested, date tested, and known failure cases. Keep credentials and private data out of prompts and examples.

## People to follow

Maintain a small, evidence-led list of researchers, builders, educators, and reporters. For each, note their expertise, useful work, and relevant interests or limits. Recheck links and claims before relying on them.
